Build a House in Goa: 2026 Legal Checklist for Plot Owners

You've bought a plot in Goa. Now comes the part most first-time owners underestimate. To build legally, you have to clear land records, zoning rules and two separate building approvals before the first brick goes in. After the last brick, you need an occupancy certificate. Miss a step and you risk refused utility connections, a rejected home loan or even a demolition notice. Here's the process in the order you'll face it.

By Kreative Solution · Reviewed by Padma Sawant, Principal Architect (COA Licensed) · September 18, 2026

Step-by-Step Checklist

  1. 1

    Verify the title and land records

    Get certified copies of Form I & XIV, Goa's record of rights, and confirm the mutation shows your name. Read the tenant and other-rights columns closely, because a tenant or mundkar entry carries protected rights, including a right to purchase, that stay with the land even after a sale. If your plot was carved out of a larger property, make sure the sub-division itself was approved. A title search by a local property lawyer is money well spent.

  2. 2

    Confirm the zone

    Ask the Town & Country Planning (TCP) department for a zoning certificate. To build a regular house, your plot generally needs to be zoned Settlement under the Regional Plan for Goa 2021 or the local Outline Development Plan. Don't buy orchard, paddy or no-development land on the promise of a future zone change. Zone changes under Sections 17(2) and 39A of the TCP Act have been challenged before the Supreme Court and the High Court.

  3. 3

    Check site-specific restrictions

    • Coastal plots: Construction within 500 m of the High Tide Line needs prior approval from the Goa Coastal Zone Management Authority (GCZMA).
    • Slopes and low-lying land: Cutting land with a gradient of 1:10 or more, or filling low-lying land, needs prior written permission from the Chief Town Planner under Section 17A. In addition, slopes steeper than 25% are treated as no-development areas.
    • Trees and forest: You need permission to fell any tree under the Goa Preservation of Trees Act, 1984. Also, land identified as private forest cannot be developed.
    • Access: How much you can build is linked to your access road. A 2022 TCP notification, for example, capped a single house reached by a traditional access of 3 m or less at 200 sq m of floor area.
  4. 4

    Secure the Conversion Sanad

    If the land is still recorded as agricultural, you need a Conversion Sanad from the Collector's office under the Goa Land Revenue Code before any construction. Check whether the seller already obtained one. If not, you can apply for TCP's recommendation for conversion along with your building application.

  5. 5

    Get TCP technical clearance

    Appoint a TCP-registered architect or engineer to design within the Goa Land Development and Building Construction Regulations, which set limits on FAR, setbacks, height and coverage. TCP, or the PDA in planning areas, issues a Technical Clearance Order after reviewing your drawings, title papers, survey plan and professional certificates. The regulations were amended again in August 2026, so make sure your plans follow the current version. Small homes have a faster route: an empanelled Notary Architect or Engineer can issue the clearance for low-risk buildings. The 2022 guidelines covered plots of up to 500 sq m in approved sub-divisions, with up to 500 sq m built-up and ground plus two storeys, stilt included.

  6. 6

    Obtain the construction licence

    With TCP clearance in hand, apply to your Village Panchayat or Municipal Council for the construction licence. You need both approvals; one alone is not enough. Add any no-objection certificates (NOCs) your site calls for, such as GCZMA for coastal plots, Health Services, or Fire in municipal areas.

  7. 7

    Build exactly as approved

    Deviations such as an extra floor or encroached setbacks can void your occupancy certificate and invite demolition. Also budget for the 1% cess on total construction cost, which you must pay before TCP issues the Completion Order.

  8. 8

    Get completion and occupancy certificates

    Your engineer certifies that the house matches the approved plans. The Panchayat then processes the Occupancy Certificate (OC) with its technical officer and TCP. Infrastructure tax applies if built-up area exceeds 100 sq m. Municipal areas follow a similar route. Don't skip this step: following a Supreme Court order, Goa has directed that electricity, water and sewerage connections be provided only after valid completion and occupancy certificates.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I build a house on orchard or agricultural land?

Usually not. A regular home needs Settlement zoning and a Sanad, so be cautious of any seller who promises the zone "will be changed."

How long do approvals take?

One guide estimates 30 to 90 days for TCP clearance plus the construction licence, assuming your documents are in order. Title issues, Sanad applications or extra NOCs can add months.

Do I need an architect?

Yes. Your plans must be prepared and certified by an architect or engineer registered with TCP.

Can I start work once TCP clears my plans?

No. Work can begin only after the Panchayat or Municipality issues your construction licence.
